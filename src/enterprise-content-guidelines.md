# ACKO for Enterprise — Content Writing & Evaluation Guidelines

---

## 1. Headlines

### What a headline must do
A headline has one job: make the reader believe one true, specific thing in under 10 words. If it takes longer, or if it could apply to any competitor, it's not working.

### Structure rules

**Lead with the outcome, not the feature**
The reader doesn't care what the product does — they care what changes for them.
- ✓ "Benefits your employees will actually use"
- ✗ "Modular group health insurance plans"
- ✓ "Claims settled before your employee leaves the hospital"
- ✗ "Fast cashless claim processing"

**One idea per headline**
Stacking claims dilutes everything.
- ✓ "Go live in 24 hours."
- ✗ "Fast claims, flexible plans, and transparent pricing for India's modern businesses"

**Active voice, present tense**
Passive voice makes benefits feel theoretical.
- ✓ "We settle claims in under 2 hours."
- ✗ "Claims are settled within the stipulated turnaround."

**Pair H1 + subhead as promise + proof**
The H1 stakes a claim. The subhead earns it.
- H1: "Benefits that run like a product."
- Sub: "Single platform. Real-time data. Zero broker dependency."

**Be specific when you can, evocative only for brand moments**
Specific: "Go live in 24 hours." (preferred for product/feature pages)
Evocative: "Insurance infrastructure, built for scale." (acceptable for brand/awareness)

### What not to do

**No question headlines in B2B**
Questions create doubt where you want confidence.
- ✗ "Is your group insurance holding you back?"
- ✓ "Your group insurance shouldn't slow you down."

**No hedging in the headline**
- ✗ "Insurance that can potentially reduce your HR burden"
- ✓ "Insurance that takes the burden off HR."

**No claims every competitor makes without proof**
- ✗ "Fast claims." (every insurer says this)
- ✓ "Claims settled in under 2 hours. On average." (specific, verifiable)

## 2. Subheads and Section Titles

### What they must do
Section titles orient the reader before they read. If a section title only makes sense after reading the section — rewrite it.

### Rules

**Name the benefit, not the feature**
- ✓ "How we make claims 3x faster"
- ✗ "Our Technology"
- ✓ "One place to manage every policy, every employee"
- ✗ "The Dashboard"

**Work as a standalone sentence**
Pull any section title out of context. Does it still make sense? If not, rewrite.
- ✗ "The ACKO Edge" (meaningless alone)
- ✓ "What you get that you don't get anywhere else"

**Parallel structure across sections on the same page**
If three sections on a page use verb-led titles, all three should. Inconsistency reads as carelessness.
- ✓ "Enroll faster." / "Claim easier." / "Report better."
- ✗ "Faster enrollment" / "Claims that are easier" / "Better reporting tools"

**No clever for clever's sake at section level**
Wit is welcome. Obscurity is not. If someone has to pause to decode a section title, it failed.

## 3. Body Copy

### The structure
Every body copy block should follow this sequence:

**Benefit → Proof → So-what**
1. State the benefit (what changes for the reader)
2. Give the proof (why they should believe it)
3. Land the so-what (why it matters to them specifically)

Example:
> "Claims settle in under 2 hours [benefit] — from the moment an employee submits through the app, with no follow-up calls needed [proof]. That's your HR team out of the claims loop entirely [so-what]."

### Sentence and paragraph rules

- Max 25 words per sentence before breaking it into two
- 2–3 sentences per paragraph for digital surfaces
- One idea per paragraph — never pack two thoughts into one block
- Short sentences earn their place by creating rhythm and emphasis. Use them deliberately.

### Opener rules

Never start with throat-clearing — a sentence that warms up before saying anything.
- ✗ "At ACKO, we believe that insurance should work for you."
- ✓ "Insurance should work for you. Here's how we make it."
- ✗ "In today's fast-paced business environment, employee benefits have never been more important."
- ✓ "Your employees notice when benefits don't work. They remember when they do."

### Feature-to-benefit rule

Every feature mentioned in body copy must earn its place with an outcome. No feature dumps.

| Feature | What it needs |
|---|---|
| "Single dashboard" | → "No chasing vendors for a monthly spreadsheet." |
| "API-ready platform" | → "Plugs into your existing HRMS. No manual data entry." |
| "24x7 claims support" | → "Employees don't call HR. They call us." |
| "Real-time data" | → "Answer leadership's cost questions on the spot." |

### What not to write

- No hedging: "may help reduce", "can potentially", "aims to provide"
- No passive benefit claims: "Costs may be reduced" → "Costs go down."
- No generic empathy without specificity: "We understand how hard insurance admin can be" is nothing. "Most HR teams spend 3–4 hours a week on insurance queries they shouldn't need to handle" is something.
- No feature-first writing for external audiences

## 4. CTAs

### The job
A CTA tells the reader exactly what happens when they click — not what they're clicking on. "Submit" is a click. "Get your custom quote" is a next step.

### Matching CTA to buyer stage

| Stage | Reader state | CTA examples |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Exploring, no urgency | "See how it works" / "Explore plans" / "Watch a 2-min demo" |
| Consideration | Evaluating, comparing | "Get a custom quote" / "Talk to our team" / "Calculate your premium" |
| Decision | Ready to move | "Get started" / "Go live in 24 hours" / "Set up your plan" |

**Primary + secondary CTA pairs**
Every high-stakes page needs two CTAs: one for ready buyers, one for browsers.
- Primary: "Get a custom quote" (action, commitment)
- Secondary: "See how it works" (no commitment, lower friction)

### Rules
- One action per CTA — no ambiguity about what happens next
- Verb-led: start with what the reader does, not what ACKO does
- Specific beats generic: "Get your custom quote" > "Get started" > "Submit"
- B2B value framing over urgency: "Get your plan in a day" not "Act now!"
- CTA copy should change by page — the same "Get a quote" on every page ignores buyer stage

### What not to write
- ✗ "Submit"
- ✗ "Click here"
- ✗ "Learn more" (as a primary CTA)
- ✗ "Don't miss out" (urgency tricks don't work in B2B)

## 5. Error States, Empty States, and Help Text

### Error states
The three things every error message must do: say what happened, say why (if known), say what to do next. One sentence each. Never blame the user.

**Structure:** [What happened] + [Why, if useful] + [What to do]

Templates:
- "Something went wrong on our end. Try again, or [contact support / reload the page]."
- "We couldn't verify that. Double-check [specific field] and try again."
- "This field is required. [Plain description of what goes here.]"
- "Your session has expired. Log in again to continue."

What not to write:
- ✗ "Invalid input" (what's invalid? why?)
- ✗ "An error occurred" (no recovery path)
- ✗ "Please ensure all mandatory fields have been filled in correctly" (stiff, no specificity)

### Empty states
An empty state is an opportunity to orient and guide, not just signal absence.

**Structure:** [What's missing] + [What to do to fill it] + [Why it matters, optional]

- ✓ "No employees added yet. Add your first employee to activate their cover."
- ✗ "No records found."
- ✓ "No claims this month. When an employee files, you'll see status and timelines here."
- ✗ "No data available."

### Help text and tooltips
Explain the why, not just the what. Under 20 words. Written for someone with no insurance background.

- ✓ "Sum Insured: The max your insurer pays per year. Family members share this unless you add a floater."
- ✗ "Sum Insured: Total coverage amount."
- ✓ "Floater: One shared pool for the whole family. Cheaper than individual covers; works well when claims are spread out."
- ✗ "Floater: A type of health insurance plan."

## 6. Notifications and Nudges

### Structure by type

**Utility messages (push, WhatsApp, transactional email)**
State → Action → Reassurance
- "Your employee [Name]'s enrollment is pending. They have until [date] to complete it. [Link]"
- Not: "Hi! Just a reminder that enrollment is still open for some employees on your plan."

**Deadline nudges**
Use loss framing — what they lose by not acting, not when the deadline is.
- ✓ "3 days left to add family cover. After [date], this option won't be available until next cycle."
- ✗ "Enrollment closes on [date]. Don't forget!"

**Marketing/nurture emails**
Benefit → Proof → CTA. No preamble.
- ✓ "Most claims at ACKO settle in under 2 hours. Here's what that looks like for your employees." → [CTA]
- ✗ "At ACKO, we're committed to making insurance better for Indian businesses. We'd love to share some of the ways we do that."

### Rules
- Lead with what changed or what's needed — never with brand name
- One action per message. If there are two actions, send two messages.
- No promotional language in utility-classified messages (WhatsApp/push) — affects deliverability
- Tone for nudges: matter-of-fact, not apologetic or pushy

## 7. Terminology

### Use these / not those

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| Group health insurance | GMC (internal shorthand only) |
| Sum insured | Sum assured / coverage amount |
| Enrollment | Enrolment (single L) |
| Add-on | Rider |
| Plan (employee-facing) | Policy |
| We settle / we process | Claims are settled (passive) |
| Relationship manager | Account manager / RM |
| Getting started / Setup | Onboarding journey (employee-facing) |
| Benefits portal | Admin panel / HR dashboard |

### Never use — anywhere in external copy
- **Hassle-free** — says nothing, used by everyone
- **Seamless** — same
- **World-class / Best-in-class** — unverifiable
- **Innovative** — show it, don't say it
- **Holistic** — vague
- **Leverage** (as a verb) — corporate-speak
- **Synergy / Synergistic** — never
- **Solution** (as a standalone noun: "our solution") — use the actual product name

## 8. Evaluation

### How to use this
Run every enterprise content asset through this before it ships. Each section is a gate. If something fails a gate, fix it before moving on.

---

### Gate 1 — Clarity
Does the content do its job fast?

- [ ] Key message understood in under 10 seconds?
- [ ] Does the headline work without the subhead?
- [ ] Does the subhead add proof to the headline's promise?
- [ ] Is every insurance term explained for a non-insurance reader?
- [ ] Is every feature paired with a user-facing outcome?
- [ ] Is there one clear next action?

**Fail condition:** Reader has to work to understand what's being offered or why it matters.

---

### Gate 2 — Writing quality
Is the craft right?

- [ ] Benefit → proof → so-what structure present in body copy?
- [ ] No sentences over 25 words without a break?
- [ ] No throat-clearing openers?
- [ ] Active voice throughout — especially in benefit statements?
- [ ] No hedging language ("may", "can potentially", "aims to")?
- [ ] No feature dumps without outcomes?

**Fail condition:** Copy is technically correct but reads flat, abstract, or corporate.

---

### Gate 3 — Voice and tone
Does it sound like ACKO Enterprise?

- [ ] Direct and warm — not sales-y, not stiff?
- [ ] No exclamation marks in B2B-facing copy?
- [ ] None of the banned words (hassle-free, seamless, world-class, holistic, etc.)?
- [ ] Tone calibrated to the right audience? (CFO copy ≠ HR copy ≠ employee copy)
- [ ] No generic empathy without specificity?

**Fail condition:** Could have been written by any insurer, broker, or SaaS company. No distinctive voice.

---

### Gate 4 — Structure and completeness
Is everything where it needs to be?

- [ ] H1 + subhead work as a standalone pair?
- [ ] Section titles name the benefit, not the feature?
- [ ] Every section earns its place — no filler sections?
- [ ] For product/portal copy: error states and empty states covered?
- [ ] CTAs matched to buyer stage?
- [ ] Primary and secondary CTA pair present on high-stakes pages?

**Fail condition:** Missing sections, wrong CTAs for context, or gaps in edge case coverage.

---

### Gate 5 — The benchmarks test
Read the copy once more. Ask:

- Would **Razorpay** put this on their site? (B2B trust, clean, outcome-led)
- Would **Lemonade** send this email? (Human, direct, no jargon)
- Would **Stripe** write this headline? (Minimal, precise, premium)

If none of them would — identify what's off (tone? specificity? structure?) and rewrite that element.

---

### Quality tiers

| Tier | Criteria | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Ship** | Passes all 5 gates. Specific, calibrated, voice-right. | Publish |
| **Revise** | Passes 3–4 gates. Usually needs: stronger headline, better audience calibration, or a tighter CTA. | One-pass edit |
| **Rethink** | Fails Gate 1 or Gate 3. Generic, wrong audience, or features without outcomes. | Structural rewrite |
| **Block** | Banned words, passive voice throughout, unverifiable claims, or no recovery path on error states. | Do not publish |
